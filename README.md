# econ1626-labour-policy-proposal

**Author: JIANG ZI JIAN**

## Article Overview:

This article discusses the impact of AI autonomous driving on Uber driver jobs and corresponding response policies. AI will reshape the driver industry by automating driving tasks, restructuring and dividing job content, strengthening algorithm management, and changing market demand. In the short term, complex road conditions and passenger services will still require manual drivers, but in the medium and long term, fixed routes, high-frequency orders, and high-profit periods may be the first to be replaced by AI autonomous driving, resulting in lower driver incomes, more unstable jobs, and greater impact on immigrants, ethnic minorities, middle-aged and elderly people, and low-educated workers. 

The article proposes three policy options: first, rapid deployment of AI autonomous driving to maintain market leadership, but the lack of protection for existing drivers may lead to public opinion and regulatory risks; second, provide retraining and employment services for drivers, but the effect is limited if there is no income security; Third, establish an autonomous driving transformation contract, including advance notice and 3 to 6 months of income subsidies, vocational training, internal priority recruitment, and revenue sharing. The authors recommend that Uber adopt a third option because it strikes a better balance between business efficiency, labor fairness, brand reputation, and regulatory acceptance.
