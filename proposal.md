# Regarding Uber's CEO's policy proposal: Transforming to respond to the impact of AI autonomous driving on Uber drivers' jobs and replacement.
## Simple Executive Summary:
AI is changing daily work through four channels: automating tasks, using algorithms to enhance management, restructuring job tasks, and changing market demand through cost reduction and efficiency gain. For Uber, the first and most direct impact of AI autonomous driving is the Uber driver position. In the short term, AI autonomous driving will first reduce the human driving demand of some time periods and routes (such as fixed routes to school and work), and in the medium and long term, AI autonomous driving may replace Uber drivers in pick-up and some complex road surface scenarios at night Whether it is worker dissatisfaction and brand risk and declining revenue, the proposal will propose three policy options: rapid deployment of market leadership and the provision of training and employment services, and finally the establishment of an AI autonomous driving transformation contract.
## Problem definition and background: How AI is changing the jobs of Uber drivers and why it must be addressed now.
Today, AI is not just about simply replacing jobs, but more often about reshaping jobs. For the ride-hailing industry like Uber, there are at least four channels of change:
1.	Automated core tasks: Autonomous driving systems directly replace the core activity in the role of "driving" (directly replacing Uber drivers).
2.	Task reassignment: Uber drivers no longer perform all driving tasks, but new jobs will shift to remote assistance, fleet scheduling, vehicle cleaning and maintenance, safety annotation, and abnormal location handling. 
3.	Algorithmic management enhancements: AI is already being used for dispatching, pricing, routing, and performance monitoring, which will change the revenue structure of Uber drivers before it is fully automated. 
4.	Market expansion and concentration: AI autonomous driving can reduce unit costs, improve supply stability, and expand travel demand, but the new revenue may flow more to the Uber platform itself and high-skilled technical positions than to the original drivers. 
The reason why policies need to be formulated now is because the commercialization of autonomous driving has moved from experiments to local areas. Uber needs to be motivated to reduce long-term costs and improve supply reliability through AV (Automated Vehicle) on the one hand, and on the other hand, it must also manage the workforce transformation well, otherwise it will face tightening local regulations, deteriorating public opinion (hating AI) and the number of drivers and loss.
